## The Parts Between Your Reader and the Merchant
The network credits the sale. Everything before it happens on your page, down to the button that sends each reader on.

**A tracking ID on every button**  
Name each spot on the page and Emergent gives its button a sub ID, such as stove-top. Where a network reports by sub ID, as impact.com does, you see which spot drove conversions.

**Comparison tables built from your notes**  
Paste test notes for each product and Emergent turns them into a table with one row per item and your verdict next to the price. Sort by price and each tagged button moves too.

**One disclosure, placed the same way**  
The FTC's Endorsement Guides ask for a disclosure that is hard to miss. Write your sentence once, and Emergent sets it under the headline of every review it builds for you.

**Outbound clicks saved as rows**  
Each outbound click lands as a row in a database you own, with the button, the sub ID, and the time. Read the rows by week, or send them to a spreadsheet tool over its API.

**Prices changed by one message**  
Change a price once by message, such as "Stove price is now $89." Emergent edits the table row and the button label together, and the old price is gone from the whole page.

**Your page as code you keep**  
Export the page and its click table to your GitHub repository on paid plans. Or keep it hosted on Emergent under your own domain (custom domains use credits) and edit it by prompt.

## How to Build an Affiliate Landing Page
Seven steps to an affiliate landing page that readers can trust and networks can credit, then build it with Emergent.

**01 Pick one offer and one reader**  
Choose a single product or program and the reader it suits, such as solo hikers who want a light stove. A page that recommends one thing to one kind of reader is easier to write, and easier to measure, than a roundup of a whole catalog.

**02 Read the program terms first**  
Note the commission, the credit window after a click, and which traffic sources the terms allow. Plan the page and any ads inside those limits. Keep a copy of the terms with the date you read them, in case they change later.

**03 Add what the merchant's page cannot**  
Write from use: test notes, your own photos, a comparison with the alternatives. Google's spam policies name original reviews, testing, and product comparisons as the value good affiliate pages add, and treat copied merchant descriptions as thin affiliation.

**04 Write the disclosure in plain words**  
Use a sentence nobody can misread: "I earn a commission on purchases made through these links." Leave out jargon and vague labels: the test in the FTC's guides is whether an ordinary reader understands it.

**05 Lead with the verdict**  
Readers who arrive from search want the answer first. Put a two-line verdict and the main button near the top, with the reason the product won. Testing notes and the full comparison go underneath for readers who want to check your reasoning.

**06 Test every link before launch**  
Open each button in a private browser window and confirm the merchant page loads with your tracking ID in the address bar. Then look for the test click in the network dashboard. A broken parameter loses the credit for every sale that follows it.

**07 Compare clicks with sales each week**  
If your network reports conversions by sub ID, set the click table against that report one sub ID at a time. A button with plenty of clicks and few sales points at the offer or the price. A button nobody clicks points at where it sits. Change one thing, then compare again.

## Affiliate Page Questions, Answered
**What is an affiliate landing page?**  
An affiliate landing page is a page an affiliate publishes between a traffic source, such as a search result or a video, and the merchant's sales page. Its one job is a click on the tracking link. ClickBank calls this a bridge page, because the reader crosses it on the way to the offer.
<sub>Source: Definition: “affiliate landing page”</sub>

**How do you create an affiliate landing page?**  
Start with one offer and the evidence only you have, such as test notes. Then describe the page to Emergent's AI landing page builder with your links and disclosure sentence. It builds the page, tags each button, and saves every click to a table.

Proof that the writer used the product: original photos, measurements, and a list of what it was tested against. They use few buttons and put each one where a decision gets made. Galleries of affiliate marketing landing page examples mostly show layout; borrow the structure from them, never the copy.

**What should an affiliate marketing landing page include?**  
A headline that names the product and who it suits, then evidence such as test notes or a comparison table. Add a plain disclosure, and give each button a sub ID of its own. Pages about a costly product also earn a short section on who should skip it.
<sub>Source: Secondary keyword: “affiliate marketing landing page”</sub>

**What is an affiliate program landing page used for?**  
A merchant uses it to recruit affiliates. The page states the commission, the credit window, and the traffic it accepts, then takes applications. On a page built with Emergent, each application lands in a database you own. A thank you page can then tell applicants when to expect an answer.
<sub>Source: Secondary keyword: “affiliate program landing page”</sub>

**Where do you add the disclosure on affiliate landing pages?**  
Near the top, before the first link a reader can click, and again beside any link far down the page. For web pages, the FTC's Endorsement Guides say a disclosure should be unavoidable. A disclosure tucked behind a link fails that test; keep the sentence itself on the page.
<sub>Source: Secondary keyword: “affiliate landing pages”</sub>

**How do you track clicks on affiliate marketing landing pages?**  
Give each link a sub ID that names the page and the button, such as stove-specs. If your network accepts sub IDs, as impact.com does, its reports show which value drove the most conversions. Log every outbound click on your own side too, and you can see which buttons draw clicks that never turn into sales.
<sub>Source: Secondary keyword: “affiliate marketing landing pages”</sub>

**Can you make affiliate landing pages without a website?**  
Yes. A single page at its own address can carry the review and its tracked buttons with no blog behind it. Emergent can host it on a custom domain, which uses credits, and on paid plans the code exports to your GitHub repository. Add a 404 page that sends readers of broken links back to the review.
<sub>Source: Secondary keyword: “affiliate landing pages”</sub>

**How do you set up links on a landing page for affiliate marketing?**  
Add rel=sponsored to the anchor tag of every merchant link, buttons included. Google asks sites in affiliate programs to mark these links, whether they were placed by hand or generated by a script. Links to your own pages stay as they are.
